## 1. Sparsity / occurrence (descriptive + Jeffreys 95% CI)

Source: `tables/01_sparsity_occurrence_by_group.csv`. NE (not a dropout
candidate) is reported separately from evaluated-zero throughout, as required.

- **NE fraction** (of the 5,718-reaction full-network union): 26.1%
  (GSE126848/healthy) to 39.5% (GSE89632/steatosis); Jeffreys 95% CI width
  ≈1-2.5 percentage points per group.
- **Evaluated-nonzero fraction** (δ_obj>τ in ≥1 of 800 backgrounds, of the
  group's own eligible pool): 4.0%-8.3%. Lowest: GSE89632/steatosis (3.90%,
  CI [3.29%,4.59%]). Highest: GSE126848/healthy (8.30%, CI [7.50%,9.16%]).
- **Feasibility-flip fraction** (of evaluated): 0.13%-2.0%, always far below
  the nonzero fraction — flips are a minority behavior within an already-rare
  detection event.

**Assumptions/limitations**: Jeffreys CIs here treat each group's reactions as
exchangeable Bernoulli trials for the *proportion-with-uncertainty* estimate
only — this is a standard descriptive-uncertainty device, not a claim that
reactions are statistically independent (they share network/pathway
structure; see Section 9 discipline note). The per-reaction occ_obj_p Jeffreys
CIs already stored in `reaction_scores.csv` are on firmer ground (800
independent Monte Carlo background draws per reaction).

## 3. Cross-group recurrence + eligibility-aware permutation null

Source: `tables/03a_reaction_eligibility_and_detection_frequency.csv`,
`03b_recurrence_null_summary.csv`; Figure C.

**Null construction** (5,000 replicates, RNG seed 20260929, fixed and
documented): for each group g independently, a random subset of exactly
n_detected(g) reactions is drawn *without replacement from that group's own
eligible pool* (size n_evaluated(g)) and marked "detected" in that
replicate. This exactly preserves, for every replicate: (a) each reaction's
true eligibility pattern across groups (ineligible reactions can never be
drawn), and (b) each group's exact observed detection count. Only *which*
eligible reactions are detected is randomized, independently per group.

| k (groups detected) | observed | null mean | null 95% band | effect size z | empirical p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 4,926 | 3,950.4 | [3,921, 3,981] | 65.2 | <0.001 |
| 1 | 308 | 1,432.4 | [1,378, 1,484] | -41.9 | <0.001 |
| 2 | 192 | 292.1 | [266, 319] | -7.4 | <0.001 |
| 3 | 84 | 39.2 | [28, 51] | 7.7 | <0.001 |
| 4 | 54 | 3.6 | [0, 8] | 26.6 | <0.001 |
| 5 | 51 | 0.22 | [0, 1] | 108.8 | <0.001 |
| 6 | 33 | 0.011 | [0, 0] | 310.7 | <0.001 |
| 7 | 39 | 0.0006 | [0, 0] | 1592.5 | <0.001 |
| 8 | 19 | 0 | [0, 0] | undefined (null_sd=0) | <0.001 |
| 9 | 8 | 0 | [0, 0] | undefined | <0.001 |
| 10 | 4 | 0 | [0, 0] | undefined | <0.001 |

The observed distribution is substantially heavier-tailed than the
eligibility-aware independence null at every recurrence level k≥3: e.g. 84
reactions observed detected in exactly 3 groups vs. a null expectation of
39.2 (95% band 28-51); 792 reactions total detected in ≥1 group vs. the
null's ≈1,768 expected in exactly-1 plus smaller tail (the null concentrates
almost all of its "≥1" mass at k=1, essentially never reaching k≥5). This is
a numerical/statistical fact about detection-pattern structure, reported
without biological interpretation.

**Assumptions/limitations**: the null models each group's detection process
as *within-group exchangeable* (uniform over its eligible pool) and *between-
group independent* given eligibility. It does not model any positive
dependence a real biological/network mechanism might induce between groups
beyond what shared eligibility alone contributes — so the null is
conservative in the sense that it isolates "does recurrence exceed what
eligibility overlap alone would produce," not "does recurrence exceed some
richer structural null." k=8,9,10 have null_sd=0 (5,000/5,000 replicates
landed at exactly 0), so z is undefined; the empirical p (fraction of
replicates ≥ observed) is still valid and is <0.0002 at each of those levels.

## 4. Cross-cohort reproducibility (matched disease states)

Source: `tables/04_cross_cohort_reproducibility.csv`; Figure D. 9 pairs across
3 matched states (healthy, steatosis, NASH each present in all 3 cohorts;
obese_no_NAFLD has no cross-cohort match and is excluded by construction).

- **Detection association** (Fisher exact test on the 2x2 joint-eligible
  detected/not-detected table): odds ratios 11.0-75.8, all p<1e-19 — reactions
  detected in one cohort are substantially more likely to also be detected in
  the matched-state cohort than chance would predict.
- **Detected-set overlap** (Jaccard, both-detected / either-detected): only
  0.17-0.43 — most of the "either detected" set is cohort-specific (only-A or
  only-B), not shared.
- **Rank concordance restricted to the "either detected" subset** (directly
  addressing the tied/null mass, as required): Spearman ranges -0.32 to
  +0.10 — near zero to modestly negative. Mechanistically: within the "either
  detected" subset, rows split into "both detected" (high rank in both),
  "only-A" (high in A, ~zero in B), and "only-B" (high in B, ~zero in A); when
  only-A + only-B outnumber both-detected (true in 7/9 pairs here), this
  structurally pulls the subset's rank correlation toward zero or negative
  even though detection status itself is positively associated (previous
  bullet). This is reported as a numeric/structural fact, not a biological
  claim.
- **Global Pearson on all jointly-eligible reactions** (not restricted to
  detected): 0.14-0.95 — high in most pairs, but this is magnitude-driven and
  dominated by the same small set of large-effect reactions in both cohorts;
  it does not by itself indicate agreement on which reactions are
  detected/ranked highly (see Jaccard/Spearman-detected above).
- **Kendall tau** (all jointly-eligible): 0.007-0.14, consistently small
  given the very large tied-at-near-zero mass — reported for completeness but
  not treated as informative on its own.

**Assumptions/limitations**: Fisher's exact test assumes the 2x2 table's
margins characterize independence correctly under exchangeable reactions;
reactions are not independent draws (shared network structure), so the
extremely small p-values should be read as "far more association than a
naive independence model would produce," not as a formally calibrated
hypothesis test in the classical iid sense.

## 5. Biomarker/calibration reactions (25 curated, NE separated)

Source: `tables/05a_biomarker_per_reaction_summary.csv` (per-reaction),
`05b_biomarker_permutation_by_group.csv` (primary inference),
`05c_biomarker_combined_stouffer.csv` (combined), `05b_exploratory_mannwhitney_only.csv`
(exploratory only, per instruction). Figure E.

- **Eligibility**: **13 of 25** biomarker reactions are **NE in all 10
  groups** — never a dropout candidate anywhere in this production run:
  MAR00636, MAR00638, MAR00653, MAR01603 (all 4 choline_pc reactions),
  MAR03744, MAR03747, MAR03765, MAR03777, MAR03778, MAR06416, MAR06923 (7 of
  9 bcaa reactions), MAR03845 (1 of 2 serine_glycine_shmt), MAR08426 (1 of 6
  urea_cycle). The remaining 12 reactions are eligible in 3-10 of the 10
  groups (exact counts in `05a_biomarker_per_reaction_summary.csv`).
- **Detection (recurrence)**: of the reactions that are ever eligible, only
  MAR03809 (urea_cycle, 2/10), MAR03873 (urea_cycle, 1/10), and MAR04792
  (serine_glycine_shmt, 2/10) show any detected (δ>τ) event at all. All other
  eligible biomarker reactions are detected in 0 groups.
- **Primary inference — eligibility-aware permutation** (5,000 reps per
  group, drawing m_g reactions from that group's own eligible pool, m_g =
  the number of biomarker reactions eligible in that group, ranging 6-11):
  **no group reaches p<0.05** (range 0.060-0.980); BH-FDR q-values are
  0.60-1.00 across all 10 groups. Detection-count hypergeometric test (exact,
  no simulation): only 3 groups have any biomarker detection at all
  (GSE89632 NASH/healthy/steatosis, 1-2 detections), all p≥0.056, BH-q≥0.39.
- **Combined test**: Stouffer combination of the 10 per-group magnitude
  z-scores (equal weight) gives Z=-0.53, p=0.60 — **no evidence, under this
  eligibility-aware design, that the 25 curated biomarker reactions behave
  differently from a random equal-sized draw from each group's own eligible
  pool.**
- **Exploratory-only Mann-Whitney** (uncorrected, retained per instruction
  as exploratory): 2/10 groups nominally p<0.05 (GSE135251/NASH p=0.0098,
  GSE89632/steatosis p=0.034). **These do not survive BH-FDR correction**
  (effectively subsumed by the permutation result above) and are reported
  here only because the instruction required keeping them visible as
  exploratory, not as evidence.

**Assumptions/limitations**: the permutation null draws from each group's
full eligible pool uniformly at random, which is the most defensible
"eligibility-aware" comparator available from these outputs, but it does not
control for any structured property of the eligible pool itself (e.g.
pathway proximity, reaction connectivity) that might differ systematically
from the biomarker set — no such annotation was loaded here (out of scope,
would begin to shade into biological interpretation).

## 6. Monte Carlo precision

Source: `tables/06_monte_carlo_precision.csv`; Figure F2.

- **SE relative to magnitude** (median of SE/|PR_objective| among detected
  reactions): 0.41-0.72 across groups — i.e. for a typical detected reaction,
  the Monte Carlo standard error is roughly 40-70% of the point estimate's
  own magnitude. This is a real, non-trivial precision limit at M=800, not
  noise-floor artifact (restricted to the detected subset specifically to
  avoid the zero-mass distorting the ratio, per instruction).
- **Rare-event vs. well-characterized regimes**: reactions with occ_obj_k≤5
  (out of 800; 64-235 reactions per group) have median Jeffreys interval
  width ≈0.0086-0.0108; reactions with occ_obj_k≥50 (13-25 reactions per
  group) have median width ≈0.052-0.073. Counterintuitively the *well-
  characterized* (higher-k) bucket has a *wider* absolute Jeffreys width —
  because Jeffreys width for a binomial proportion is widest near p=0.5 and
  narrowest near p=0 or 1; k≤5 out of 800 sits deep in the narrow low-p
  regime, while k≥50/800 (p≈0.06-0.09+) sits closer to where the width
  function is locally larger. This is reported as-is, a direct consequence
  of binomial CI geometry, not a claim that rare events are "better
  characterized" than common ones in any absolute-precision-of-the-rate
  sense — both regimes' *relative* precision (CI width / p) actually improves
  with higher k, as expected.
- **Explicit scope statement** (repeated from every group's manifest, not
  reinterpreted): Monte Carlo SE/CI here reflect background-sampling
  variance conditional on the fixed, already-constructed context-specific
  group model. **They do not cover model-construction, consensus-algorithm,
  cross-cohort, or biological/measurement uncertainty.** No claim to the
  contrary is made anywhere in this package.

## 7. Pilot → production validation

Source: `tables/07a_pilot_vs_production_correlation.csv`,
`07b_bland_altman_source.csv`; Figure F1.

| group | n | Pearson r | Spearman r | Kendall τ | BA bias | BA 95% LoA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GSE126848/obese_no_NAFLD | 44 | 0.99957 | 0.4052 | 0.3129 | -0.0293 | [-0.247, 0.189] |
| GSE135251/healthy | 40 | 0.98198 | 0.5062 | 0.3795 | +1.04e-5 | [-2.60e-4, 2.80e-4] |

**Magnitude vs. rank reproducibility, explicitly distinguished**: Pearson
(magnitude/scale agreement) is very high in both groups (r>0.98), and
Bland-Altman bias is close to zero (production is not systematically
inflated or deflated relative to pilot at the group-mean level) — but the
limits of agreement are wide relative to the typical detected-reaction
magnitude from Section 2, meaning individual-reaction magnitude agreement is
noisier than the aggregate Pearson r alone suggests. Spearman/Kendall (rank
agreement) are only moderate (0.41-0.51 / 0.31-0.38) — consistent with
Section 1's finding that most of the 44/40-reaction curated overlap sits
near the same zero/noise floor described in Section 1, where relative order
is unstable even when the handful of large-magnitude reactions agree closely
in absolute terms between pilot and production.

**Assumptions/limitations**: pilot M_effective (120-480, pooled across 2-4
seeds) is far smaller than production's M=800, so pilot-side Monte Carlo
noise is itself substantial and not decomposed separately from production-
side noise in this Bland-Altman analysis (a two-source-of-variance
decomposition was not attempted here).

## Statistical discipline notes (Section 9)

- Every cross-reaction inferential claim in this report (Sections 3, 4, 5)
  uses permutation/randomization or exact combinatorial tests (hypergeometric,
  Fisher exact) that explicitly account for the eligibility/detection
  structure, rather than treating 3,460-4,228 candidate reactions per group as
  independent biological replicates for a parametric test.
- Multiple testing: Section 5's 10 per-group tests are BH-FDR corrected
  (q-values reported); the 10 exploratory Mann-Whitney p-values are
  explicitly labeled exploratory and superseded by the permutation result,
  not corrected-and-reported as primary.
- Descriptive vs. inferential vs. sensitivity, explicitly:
  - **Descriptive**: Section 1 (proportions+Jeffreys CI), Section 2
    (magnitude distribution+bootstrap CI), Section 6 (precision ratios).
  - **Inferential**: Section 3 (permutation null vs. observed recurrence),
    Section 4 (Fisher exact on detection association), Section 5 (primary
    permutation + Stouffer combination; hypergeometric exact test).
  - **Sensitivity/exploratory**: Section 5's retained Mann-Whitney results;
    Section 7's Pearson/Spearman/Kendall trio (three lenses on the same
    comparison, not three independent claims).
- All randomization used a single fixed, documented RNG seed (20260929) and
  5,000 replicates throughout (Sections 3 and 5), for reproducibility.

## Analyses judged scientifically invalid/uninterpretable and NOT performed

1. **Pair-level (background-level) pooled δ_objective distribution across all
   10 groups for Section 2.** Would require decompressing ~8,000 gzip pair
   shards (~20M rows); reaction-level PR_objective (already the mean over
   800 backgrounds) was used instead as the operational "positive-effect
   magnitude," documented explicitly in Section 2 rather than silently
   substituted. Not "invalid," but a scoped-out-for-runtime decision, flagged
   as requested.
2. **Naive reaction-by-reaction significance testing across all 5,718 x 10
   matrix cells** (e.g. a per-cell binomial/chi-square test against some
   global rate) — judged statistically invalid as a *primary* analysis given
   reactions are structurally dependent (shared stoichiometry/pathway
   membership), and would constitute exactly the "thousands of reactions
   treated as independent replicates" pattern the task explicitly warned
   against. The recurrence-null permutation approach (Section 3) supersedes
   this and was used instead.
3. **Any biological pathway/subsystem enrichment analysis** — no pathway
   annotation was loaded, and this would necessarily cross into biological
   interpretation, explicitly out of scope for this pass.
4. **Any combination with consensus, flux-sampling, or calibrated-confidence
   scores** — explicitly forbidden by the instructions; not performed.
5. **Formal two-source (pilot + production) variance decomposition for the
   Bland-Altman analysis in Section 7** — both runs' own Monte Carlo SE
   estimates exist (`PR_objective_se` in each `reaction_scores.csv`) but were
   not propagated into a combined-uncertainty Bland-Altman band here; the
   plotted/reported limits of agreement reflect only the observed
   pilot-vs-production differences, not a model-based decomposition. Could be
   added as a follow-up if useful.
6. **A single pooled p-value or effect size across all 9 cross-cohort pairs
   in Section 4** — the 3 disease states are structurally different
   comparisons (different reaction pools, different cohort sample sizes
   feeding upstream steps); reported per-pair rather than pooled, to avoid
   manufacturing a false sense of one summary number.
